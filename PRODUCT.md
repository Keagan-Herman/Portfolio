# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

Hiring managers and recruiters with technical authority: people deciding whether to shortlist a full-stack engineer for a product or agency role. They arrive with purpose, scan fast, and form a gut read within the first scroll. Technical peers may follow a link, but they're not the primary reader.

## Product Purpose

A single-page portfolio that convinces a decision-maker to reach out. The argument it makes: Keagan Herman is not a junior who needs hand-holding — he owns production systems, works directly with clients, and delivers across the full stack without the problem being pre-solved. The measure of success is an interview request, not a compliment on the design.

## Brand Personality

Sharp, ambitious, direct. The voice already present in the copy ("I own the outcome", "real load, real users, real consequences") is the brand. Design should carry the same register: precise, confident, slightly blunt. No softening, no hedging.

## Anti-references

- Generic developer portfolio: dark mode, neon accent colors, particle/canvas hero backgrounds, GitHub contribution graphs as hero content, terminal-text animations. Anything that reads "frontend dev showcase" rather than "engineer who ships production systems".
- SaaS landing page aesthetic: feature grids, gradient headline text, hero-metric templates with big numbers + small labels arranged in stat blocks, "supercharge / leverage / seamless" copy cadence, prominent CTA blocks with rounded pill buttons.
- Maximalist or visually chaotic: competing motion systems, overlapping type at multiple weights, loud color clashes, decorative elements fighting for attention. Ornament that exists only to demonstrate effort.

## Design Principles

1. **Consequence over activity.** Show what shipped and who uses it, not that code was written. "200+ institutions" lands harder than a bullet list of technologies.
2. **Typography as argument.** Scale, weight, and white space are the primary design tools. Adornment only where it sharpens emphasis; never purely decorative.
3. **Precision signals judgment.** The page is itself a demonstration of the engineer's taste. Inconsistency, visual clutter, or lazy defaults undermine the copy's claims.
4. **No performance.** Animations serve comprehension or create purposeful emphasis; they don't exist to impress. If a motion doesn't add meaning, cut it.
5. **Committed restraint.** One saturated accent color, used with intention. Restraint isn't sparseness; it's knowing where to commit.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG AA minimum. Reduced motion handled via `prefers-reduced-motion` (already in globals.css). No specific accommodations stated beyond standard contrast and keyboard navigation.
